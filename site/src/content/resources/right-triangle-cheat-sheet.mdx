## The Pythagorean theorem

For a right triangle with legs $a$, $b$ and hypotenuse $c$:

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

Solve for any one side given the other two:

- $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$
- $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$ (and symmetrically for $b$)

## The three basic trig ratios

For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle, relative to the sides adjacent to and opposite that angle:

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Sine | $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ |
| Cosine | $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$ |
| Tangent | $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$ |

A common mnemonic: **SOH-CAH-TOA**.

## Two special triangles worth memorizing

**45-45-90** (an isosceles right triangle): if the two legs have length $x$, the hypotenuse is $x\sqrt{2}$.

**30-60-90**: if the shortest side (opposite the 30° angle) has length $x$, the side opposite 60° is $x\sqrt{3}$, and the hypotenuse is $2x$.

Recognizing one of these two ratios instantly, without re-deriving it from trig, is one of the fastest wins available on a timed test.

## Area and perimeter

- Area: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{leg}_1 \times \text{leg}_2$ (the two legs are always perpendicular, so they work directly as base and height)
- Perimeter: $\text{leg}_1 + \text{leg}_2 + \text{hypotenuse}$
