For any quadratic equation in the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ (with $a \neq 0$):

$$
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

## The discriminant tells you what kind of roots to expect — before you finish solving

The expression under the square root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is called the **discriminant**. Its sign alone tells you the nature of the roots:

| Discriminant | Roots |
|---|---|
| $> 0$ | Two distinct real roots |
| $= 0$ | One repeated real root (the parabola touches the x-axis at exactly one point) |
| $< 0$ | Two complex conjugate roots (the parabola never crosses the x-axis) |

Checking the discriminant's sign first is a fast way to sanity-check a computed answer — if you calculated two different real roots but the discriminant is negative, something went wrong upstream.

## The vertex, from the same coefficients

The parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ has its vertex at:

$$
x = -\frac{b}{2a}, \qquad y = a\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 + b\left(-\frac{b}{2a}\right) + c
$$

The $x$-coordinate is also the axis of symmetry — the two roots (when real) are always equally spaced on either side of it.

## Worked example

For $x^2 - 3x + 2 = 0$: $a=1$, $b=-3$, $c=2$.

- Discriminant: $(-3)^2 - 4(1)(2) = 9 - 8 = 1$ — positive, so two distinct real roots.
- Roots: $x = \dfrac{3 \pm 1}{2}$, giving $x = 2$ and $x = 1$.
- Vertex: $x = \dfrac{3}{2}$, $y = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2 - 3\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right) + 2 = -\dfrac{1}{4}$.
